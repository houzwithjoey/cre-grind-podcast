## Five things to remember

1. **Priority sets risk.** Cash flows down the stack and losses hit from the top: senior debt, then mezz or pref, then common equity.
2. **Loans are sized by three tests**: LTV, DSCR and debt yield. The loan is the smallest amount that passes all three. In Q3 2026 the averages were about 64% LTV, 1.32x DSCR and a 9.5% debt yield (CBRE).
3. **Mezz and pref fill the same gap but work differently.** Mezz is a loan secured by a pledge of the equity, enforced through a fast UCC foreclosure and governed by an intercreditor agreement. Pref is equity with priority, enforced through the operating agreement. Mid-2026 pricing is roughly 11–16% for mezz and 8–15% for pref. The real difference between them is control.
4. **Alternative lenders are taking share.** They were 48% of CBRE's non-agency closings in Q3 2026, up from 37%. Banks were 26%, and often fund the debt funds through repo lines and CRE CLOs. Private-label CMBS was about $90B through August, mostly SASB deals.
5. **The market is in a "resolve or reset" phase.** The Fed hiked to 3.75–4.00% in September and held in October. CMBS delinquency was 7.87% in October and office around 12%. About $875B of loans mature in 2026, and that is drawing in rescue capital.

## The worked example

A sponsor buys a $100M Jersey City apartment building with $6.5M of NOI, a 6.5% cap rate. The stack is a $65M senior loan at 6.25% interest-only, $15M of preferred equity at 12%, and $20M of sponsor equity.

- **Senior loan:** DSCR about 1.6x and debt yield 10%, both comfortably inside today's averages.
- **Cash to common equity:** about $0.64M a year (3.2%) today. If NOI grows to $7.5M, that rises to about $1.64M (8.2%), because the senior loan and the pref take fixed returns. This is leverage at work.
- **Downside:** a 20% fall in value wipes out the common equity entirely. The senior lender still has a 35% cushion beneath it.
- **Negative leverage:** the blended cost of the debt and pref is about 7.3%, above the 6.5% cap rate. The deal only works if NOI grows.
