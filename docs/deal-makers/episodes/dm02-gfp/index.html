GFP: The Family Landlord Turning Wall Street Offices Into Homes

How the Gural family's 1952 Newmark roots became GFP Real Estate, why it split off GFP Development in 2026, and how it turned obsolete Lower Manhattan towers into SoMA (1,320 units), Wrey (788 units) and 40 Exchange Place, plus the Flatiron condos, 100 Gold Street and the Chrysler Building that got away.

Five things to remember

  1. A family business since 1952, born from the old Newmark. Aaron Gural and partners bought Newmark & Co. in 1952. Jeff Gural and Barry Gosin took over in 1978. When the brokerage was sold to BGC in 2011, the family kept the buildings, and in 2017 the ownership company became GFP: Gural Family Properties.
  2. Two companies since April 2026. GFP Real Estate (Jeff Gural, chairman and CEO; Eric Gural, principal) runs the legacy portfolio: one of New York's largest landlords to small and medium tenants and nonprofits. GFP Development (Brian Steinwurtzel, CEO) does conversions and development in NYC and Jersey City.
  3. The leader in office-to-residential conversions. SoMA at 25 Water Street (1,320 units) is the largest in the U.S., and Wrey at 222 Broadway added 788 more. 40 Exchange Place (382 units) is funded.
  4. The playbook: low basis, then an institutional equity partner (TPG, Rockwood), then private-credit construction loans (MSD, Apollo, BDT & MSD, Derby Lane), then the 467-m tax incentive, then a refinancing. At SoMA: bought for $251M, refinanced for $835M.
  5. Next: 100 Gold Street on city land (~4,000 homes, ~1,000 affordable), Jersey City, and possibly new asset types, while the 467-m clock keeps ticking.

  • In a conversion, the building is often the small number. Most of the money goes into windows, plumbing, mechanical systems, elevators, extra floors and amenities.
  • 467-m: 25% of units permanently affordable in exchange for a 90% property tax exemption: 35 years for projects started by June 30, 2026, 30 years by June 2028, and 25 years by June 2031. Unlike 485-x, there are no construction wage requirements. The tax savings raise net operating income, which supports bigger loans and higher values.
  • Risks: the physical layout (deep floor plates, too few windows), construction surprises, leasing hundreds of units at once, and the policy deadlines.

If you're interviewing there

  • Know which company you're talking to. GFP Real Estate means operating, leasing and refinancing older buildings with small tenants. GFP Development means acquisitions, entitlements, construction, financing and lease-up.
  • Know the deals with numbers: SoMA, Wrey, 40 Exchange Place and 100 Gold (see the table above).
  • Be able to explain 467-m and why it makes the math work.
  • Questions to ask: What physical features make or break a conversion? How does the firm plan for the 467-m step-downs after 2028? When does it use PE partners versus family capital? What lessons from 100 Gold apply to other public sites?
  • A nice through-line: Jeff Gural converted West Side warehouses into offices, and the next generation is converting offices into homes.
