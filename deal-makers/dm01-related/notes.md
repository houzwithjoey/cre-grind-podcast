## Five things to remember

1. **It started as a financing business.** Stephen Ross founded Related Housing Companies in 1972 with a $10,000 loan from his mother, syndicating the tax benefits of affordable housing to investors. Related is still one of the largest private owners of affordable housing in the U.S.: about 65% of the ~78,500 apartments it manages are in affordable programs.
2. **It's vertically integrated.** Development, construction management, property and asset management, affordable housing, and investment management through Related Fund Management (RFM, private equity and credit, launched around 2010), plus regional platforms and Related Digital. It reports more than $70 billion of assets owned or under development.
3. **The playbook: anchor tenant, then partners, then financing.** Time Warner at Columbus Circle; Time Warner, KKR and Wells Fargo at 30 Hudson Yards; Deloitte at 70 Hudson Yards; Oracle in Michigan.
4. **Repeat partners.** Oxford Properties at Hudson Yards, Wells Fargo as a lender over and over, Japanese investors (Mitsui Fudosan, Mori Trust) buying into towers, and now Blackstone and PIMCO in data centers.
5. **Three bets at once:** new trophy office (70 Hudson Yards), housing at both ends of the market (Willets Point, the western rail yards), and digital infrastructure (Related Digital).

## The 70 Hudson Yards math

- Total capitalization **$2.45B**, of which a **$1.6B** construction loan led by Wells Fargo, Bank of America and Standard Chartered. That leaves about **$850M of equity**, so the loan is roughly **65% of capital**.
- Deloitte takes about **800,000 of ~1.4M sf**, so about **57% of the tower was pre-leased** to one creditworthy tenant. That's what made the lending possible in a weak office market.
- The unleased ~43% is the equity's upside: lease it well and the finished tower is worth more than it cost. If leasing is slow, equity absorbs the hit before the lenders do.

## If you're interviewing there

- Explain the model in one breath: a long-term, vertically integrated owner and developer that uses partners' capital for projects bigger than its own balance sheet, plus an investment management arm.
- Know these deals with numbers: 70 Hudson Yards ($2.45B), the Michigan data center ($16B), Willets Point, and the 10 Hudson Yards refinancing (~$1.4B).
- Be ready for: *"Why would a mixed-use developer get into data centers?"* Answer with entitlements, power and infrastructure, public-private work, anchor tenants and capital raising.
- Questions to ask: How does Related decide between its own balance sheet, an RFM fund and a joint venture? How does Related Digital share people and processes with core development? How is the affordable financing mix changing? How is RFM's credit strategy positioned as banks pull back?
- Know which part of the firm you want: development, asset management, affordable housing, or RFM.
